Why Now

Research Directions

1. EDA Algorithms: Optimization & AI

Differentiable optimization, reinforcement learning, graph neural networks, ...

New algorithms for core EDA problems such as routing and layout decomposition.

Good fit if you like: algorithms, optimization, machine learning, CS theory.

Example: differentiable global routing (DAC'24).

Read more →

2. Agentic EDA

How do we bring AI agents into EDA?

Agents that read RTL, layouts, timing reports, and tool logs, call EDA tools, check their own progress, and know when to ask a human.

Good fit if you like: LLM agents, reasoning, tool use, multimodal learning.

Example: BRIDGES, connecting graph-structured chip data with LLM reasoning.

Read more →

3. Hardware Testing

Can we trust chips after fabrication?

Some defective chips pass manufacturing test and later produce wrong results in datacenters without warning (silent data corruption). We study why defects escape and how to catch them, before and after deployment.

Good fit if you like: testing, reliability, systems, working with real industrial data.

Example: ITC'22: the standard industrial fault model matched only ~5% of thousands of real failures.

Read more →

4. Future Hardware

Design automation for 3D ICs, memory systems, photonics, ...

As 2D silicon scaling slows, new hardware needs its own design and test tools. We build them together with device and architecture researchers.

Good fit if you like: architecture, emerging devices, cross-disciplinary work.

Who Should Apply

You do not need an EDA or hardware background. Many chip-design problems are optimization, graph, generative-modeling, reinforcement-learning, or agentic problems in disguise. My own undergraduate major was computer science. What matters most is curiosity, discipline, and the courage to work on problems that are larger than one person.
